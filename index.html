The end goal

A hand up, not a hand-out.

We start with gear and food today. Every donation, hour and skill moves us up the mountain towards a place where people can learn, eat and live with dignity, then step back into society.

🎒

Now: gear, clothes & food

Collect and hand out blankets, tents, backpacks, clothing, sanitary items and non-perishables.

🛠️

Next: a community skills centre

Free classes and workshops run by neighbours with skills: CVs, trades, computers, cooking and more.

🏠

Then: a halfway house

A safe, stable bed for people working towards a job and a home of their own.

🍲

Always: 3 meals a day

Breakfast, lunch and supper for everyone in the programme.

Targets are placeholders until we have real quotes. Progress is published openly.

Ubuntu

Together, we are one.

Nobody here is a “project”. The person sleeping under the bridge, the volunteer sorting blankets and the neighbour giving R50 a month are all part of one community. Ubuntu is our working rule, and it means we are only as strong as our weakest link, so we strengthen that link first.

Our core mission

Quality social accountability. Only the best.

Helping people is not an excuse for sloppy work. People in crisis deserve the same standard as paying customers. These are the promises we hold ourselves to.

🔍

Independently reviewed

Our books are reviewed each year by an independent accounting professional. Our programme is reviewed by three community reviewers who are not on our team. Every report is posted online.

🛡️

Reliable people, properly trained

Every volunteer is screened, signs our code of conduct, completes a short training and works with a buddy before working alone.

⛓️

The weakest-link rule

Each month we name our weakest area, whether that’s storage, safety, volunteers or cash, and fix it before we grow. No expansion on a shaky foundation.

🪟

Transparent by default

Ledger, policies, reviews and results are published on this page. Community-run means the community can check our work.

✍️

Original and honest

Every word, number and promise here is our own. Estimates are labelled as estimates, and nothing is copied from other organisations.

A think tank of opportunity

Not a hand-out. A way of thinking.

Warm Table View is a think tank of opportunity and collective solutions: a living, open idea that gets better every time a neighbour adds to it. We don’t do pity. We build chances, and we measure whether they work.

💡

Collective solutions

The best ideas come from the people closest to the problem: residents, volunteers and people with lived experience.

🔄

Ever-evolving

Nothing here is fixed. We test, learn, change and publish what happened.

🕊️

Non-governed

No government, party, church or single owner controls this. Decisions are made openly, by the community, under rules anyone can read.

🔓

Open source

Our code, policies and playbooks are free. Any suburb can copy them, improve them and run their own.

📈

Progressive

We aim to uplift and incentivise, and to move people forward rather than keep them where they are.

Trauma-informed care

Dignity first. Healing with professionals.

Many people on the street have lived through loss, violence or abuse. We treat everyone with that in mind, and we are honest about who can do what.

🤲

Every volunteer

Trained in trauma-informed basics: offer choice, ask don’t assume, never press for someone’s story, and protect privacy.

🩺

Counselling

Delivered only by registered professionals: psychologists, registered counsellors and social workers, working under supervision. Volunteers are never therapists.

🧭

A clear referral path

If someone is in crisis, volunteers follow a simple script and connect them to the right service.

Our rules, in the open.

Anyone can read, question and hold us to these.

Volunteer code of conduct
  • Treat everyone with dignity, whether they give or receive.
  • Work in pairs and in daylight. Never enter a private home.
  • Never give or accept cash, gifts or favours outside the programme.
  • Never take photos or share stories without written consent.
  • Keep private information private.
  • No alcohol or drugs while volunteering.
  • Report any concern to the coordinator straight away.
  • Breaking these rules ends volunteering.
Safeguarding
  • No volunteer is ever alone with a child or vulnerable person.
  • Volunteers are screened and have a probation period.
  • Concerns are recorded, reviewed and acted on.
Money rules
  • Two people sign off every payment.
  • No salaries and no profit.
  • Donations and spending are posted in the public ledger.
  • Books are independently reviewed once a year and the report is published.
Independent review
  • Financial: an independent accounting professional.
  • Programme: a panel of three community reviewers outside our team.
  • Reports are posted here, including what we got wrong.

This is a proudly Mlungu concept and prototype: an idea from an ordinary resident, built in the open and shared for everyone to improve. It isn’t a finished organisation yet. It’s a starting point, and it only becomes real if neighbours pick it up.

Questions

Good to know.

Why gear and food, not cash on the street?

Items go straight to a need. Funds are pooled to build lasting things: the centre, the house and the meals.

Is it safe to volunteer?

Yes. We work in pairs, in daylight and in public places, and we never enter homes.

Are you a registered organisation?

Not yet. We’re a prototype, and we’ll complete the legal registration needed before we take any funds.
